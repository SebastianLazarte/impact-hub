## Nadia, safety surveyor (sales engineer)
Evidence: assumption; role and survey content from public material (R3, R4)

- Context: visits warehouses and plants, or guides a customer by video, to find where
  vehicles can hit people, racking or machinery. Writes the specification afterwards.
- Goals: a specification that stands up to the customer's safety manager; finish the
  write-up the same day.
- Frustrations today: recalculating energies by hand for every changed fact; losing
  track of which facts were guessed; a barrier that turns out not to fit the gap.
- Comfort with software: daily user of a tablet and spreadsheets; not a developer.
- Would invalidate this persona: surveyors already have a tool that computes energy per
  point, or the energy calculation is done only by a central engineering team.

## Tomás, customer health and safety manager
Evidence: assumption

- Context: responsible for a site with forklifts and pedestrians; signs off spending on
  safety and answers to auditors after an incident.
- Goals: know why each barrier was chosen; be able to show the reasoning to an auditor.
- Frustrations today: specifications that list products without the numbers behind them.
- Comfort with software: reads reports; enters only the site facts he is asked for
  (vehicle speeds, loads) when nobody can measure them.
- Would invalidate this persona: buyers choose on price alone and never read the
  justification.

## Elena, safety engineer
Evidence: assumption

- Context: works in the engineering team that backs the surveyors. Checks the cases a
  standard barrier does not settle and designs solutions outside the catalog.
- Goals: see at once which points need her and why; sign only what she has checked;
  leave a record that holds up after an incident.
- Frustrations today: specifications reach her late, mixed with routine ones, with no
  mark of which facts were measured and which were guessed.
- Comfort with software: daily user of engineering tools; reads numbers closely.
- Would invalidate this persona: surveyors sign every specification themselves, or no
  engineer reviews specifications before they reach a customer.
